# dragon-realm

A 15–90 s film about a legendary creature in a landscape, in the look of a game-engine cinematic, that is **one HTML
file**: three.js r170 inlined, no add-ons, no images, models, fonts, HDRIs or sound files, no network. Geometry, textures,
shaders and the whole soundtrack are computed when the page opens. A film is a folder of small source files (a beat
sheet, the land, the creature, the stage, the weather, the score, the shots); `build.mjs` joins them with the engine
into `index.html`, which loops live in a browser (click for sound, space pauses) and is rendered frame by frame to MP4.

Made from the film in <https://github.com/ChetasLua/crimson-loong> (赤龍 · Crimson Loong by Chetaslua: a red loong and a
white loong over a karst valley, 90 s, "pure three.js, no assets"), shared at
<https://x.com/chetaslua/status/2121176151234330707?a=fPtJuQnZ47fP-u87Nkc&s=19>. That repository has no licence, so
**none of its code is here**: its methods were studied (`references/SOURCE.md`, `references/LOOK.md`) and this engine was
written from scratch around them, with a different frame graph (depth-aware ray-marched air instead of baked sky layers,
texture-skinned body, cut shots). Its story, its two dragons and its valley are its own — **never recreate them**.
Built and proven in this repo on `examples/cheongryong` 「청룡 · 여의주」 (84 s, 17 shots): the Korean answer the request
asked for — an imugi waits in a mountain lake under five granite peaks, takes the yeouiju in a storm and becomes the Blue
Dragon at sunrise; a lake pavilion in full dancheong, red pines and maples, daegeum, gayageum, janggu and jing.
`starter/` (Tarn Wyrm, 14 s) is a second, unrelated topic.

**Fixed by the skill (the style)**
- One file, pure three.js, zero assets, zero network; sound synthesised off-line in a worker.
- **A frame is a pure function of film time.** No simulation, no state between frames: closed forms, seeded numbers,
  tables built at load. Live playback and the MP4 are the same pictures.
- The picture: a real landscape at film scale — jointed rock, water that mirrors, weather that moves — in HDR with one
  shadow-casting key light, analytic sky fill, air (haze + valley mist + ray-marched clouds), bloom, sun shafts, ACES,
  a grade, grain. Saturated, lit, deep: a trailer, not a diagram.
- The hero: one long-bodied creature on one rail; the world answers it (wake, splash, spray, leaves, swinging lanterns).
- The grammar: a beat sheet on a musical clock; picture and score read the same times; establishing tableau → omen →
  eruption → climax with elemental light → release → a last tableau that can loop.
- 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps; a body audit and a cast sheet for the creature; `check.mjs` must pass.

**Designed for every topic (never reused from the example or the starter)**
- The legend and its beats; the culture: its rock, its trees, its buildings and their painted or carved ornament, its
  instruments, its scale and rhythm.
- The land: the height function, every massif, the water, the falls; the looks (hours and weather) and their timeline.
- The creature: proportions, head (snout, brow, horns, whiskers, mane, legs or none), both palettes, what it does and
  whether it changes; its route, speed and state through the film.
- Every shot (lens, move, what it shows), every effect and when, the theme of the score and every sound cue.

## Project layout (what you create)
```
my-film/
  BRIEF.md            written first (references/BRIEF.md)
  film.json           { "title", "lang", "bg" }
  src/00_beats.js     the beat sheet: const B = { … }, BAR, BEAT
  src/10_world.js     height function, massifs, water, falls
  src/20_flora.js     this world's species            (optional)
  src/30_<culture>.js this culture's buildings        (optional)
  src/40_creature.js  the creature: route, state, parts
  src/50_weather.js   events: lightning, splash, sparks, wakes, leaves
  src/60_stage.js     where buildings stand and plants grow
  src/80_score.js     DR.score(function (K, B, o) { … })
  src/90_film.js      DR.light(looks, timeline), shots, DR.film({ … })   ← always last
  index.html          built (the film)
  out/                stills, sheets, score.wav, film.mp4
```
Sources are plain scripts joined in name order into one module: a later file sees the earlier ones; no import/export.

## Requirements
Node 18+ with Playwright Chromium, ffmpeg. No npm install, no GPU, no keys. (Without a GPU a 1080p frame of the example
takes 3–6 s on four cores; the page itself wants a real GPU to play live.)

## Workflow
1. **Topic → brief.** Read `references/BRIEF.md` and write `BRIEF.md` in the project: the legend in one line, the
   culture sheet (rock, trees, buildings, ornament, instruments, mode, rhythm), the beat sheet with times, the looks,
   the creature sheet, the shot list. Research what you do not know (what the roofs really look like, which tree, which
   drum pattern): the brief is where "East Asian" becomes Korean, or "Norse" becomes a stave church.
2. **Start the folder.** `node dragon-realm/scripts/new-film.mjs my-film` and write `src/00_beats.js` from the brief.
   Pick a tempo so that beats fall on whole or half seconds.
3. **Land and light first.** `10_world.js` + the looks in `90_film.js` with two or three static shots. Render
   `--sheet --scale .33` and judge the empty world: silhouette of the rock, where the water is, where the light comes
   from in each look. Fix the land before anything stands on it.
4. **The creature alone.** `40_creature.js`: proportions, palettes, parts. Render `--cast` and go through
   `references/CREATURE.md` (front, both 3/4, both sides, above, below, behind: one animal from every side?). Then lay the
   route against the beats and run `check.mjs`: no kinks, no flips, never backwards.
5. **Stage, weather, shots.** Buildings and planting (`60_stage.js`: keep sight lines and the creature's low passes
   clear), events on the beats (`50_weather.js`), then every shot. One move per shot; follow the head with a smoothed
   target; pass `ground` to `DR.film` so the lens cannot sink into the land.
6. **Score.** `80_score.js` against the same `B`; `node scripts/score.mjs my-film` prints loudness per section — the
   quiet opening should sit 10+ LU under the climax; hits land on their beats.
7. **Verify.** `check.mjs` (must print `check OK`), `--sheet` (every shot at its start, middle and end) scored with the
   review in `references/BRIEF.md`, `--cast`, then stills at full size of the three most important frames. Fix and repeat.
8. **Render.** `node scripts/render.mjs my-film` → `out/film.mp4` (1920×1080, with the score). Start it in the
   background; it resumes with `--resume`. Open `index.html` in a browser for the live film.

## Rules
- **Design for the topic.** The example's peaks, pavilion, pines, palette, route and tune belong to its legend. A new
  topic gets its own height function, massifs, species, buildings, creature and score; reuse the kit, not the designs.
- **One style.** Whatever you add obeys the same rules: made by code at load, lit by the shared light and air
  (`DR.mat`), a pure function of `t`, at film scale in metres.
- **No assets, no network, one file.** No loaders, no `data:` URIs, no fonts (draw glyph strokes if you need writing).
- **Nothing remembers the last frame.** No `Math.random`, no clocks, no accumulators in frame functions; anything that
  seems to need memory is a closed form of `t − t0` or a table built at load. Pose creatures before lights that follow them.
- **Texture look-ups inside loops or branches use an explicit level** (`textureLod`): automatic mip selection is
  undefined there and shows up as frames that differ from themselves.
- **The body must be possible.** Rail turns stay wider than 2.2 × the body radius; keys advance along the route; the
  head faces the way it moves; limbs stay inside their ranges (`check.mjs` enforces the first three).
- **The lens stays out of the ground** and far enough from the creature to see it (a body 2 m thick wants ≥ 15 m).
- **Light by looks, not by tweaks in shots.** A change of key-light direction lands on a cut.
- **Budget.** On a CPU rasteriser triangles and overdraw cost time: massifs at two densities, plants with three levels of
  detail chosen once at load, small plants out of the mirror and the shadow pass, the mirror off in shots without water.
- **Never put the MP4 or `out/` in a repository.**

## Contract
- `window.film = { DURATION, FPS, renderAt(t), canvas, shots, runChecks(), cast(i), info(), audio }`, `window.READY`
  after two warm-up frames; `?render=1&scale=…` fixes the size and stops the clock; `?off=air,mirror,shadow,fx,flora,audio`
  switches parts off for timing; `?t=` starts live playback at a time.
- `renderAt(t)`: frame functions with order < 50 (creatures) → light → camera → frame functions ≥ 50 → shadow map →
  mirror view → world + air → effects → post. Same `t`, same pixels.
- A shot: `{ name, t0, cam(u, t) → { p, l, fov, roll }, shadow: { span, at }, shake, mirror, near }`.
- A look: any of the fields in `lib/20_sky.js` `DEF`; `DR.light(looks, [[t, name, ease], …])` blends them.
