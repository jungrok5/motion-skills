# LOOK — what makes it read as this style

Observed from the reference: OscarAI (@Artedeingenio) on X, 「The Girl Who Drew a Door — When a Drawing Discovers the Real
World」 — https://x.com/Artedeingenio/status/2122359802328207699?a=tNv0nx5KaAk-8vSz8XU&s=19 (30 s, 1280×720, an
AI-generated clip described by its author as "mixed-media animation, combining photorealistic live-action environments
with delicate, traditional graphite pencil animation"). Stills were taken one a second; what follows is the grammar and
the medium, not the clip's characters. Never recreate its girl, door, stairs, flower, butterfly or heart.

## Two worlds in one frame
| | the real world | the drawing |
|---|---|---|
| what | a photographed place (a cobbled street, a café table, a spiral sketchbook, a hand with a pencil) | one small character and the things drawn for it |
| how it is made here | Poly Haven CC0 scans: an HDRI (light + backdrop), scanned surfaces, scanned objects; built things (table, page, pencil) from numbers | graphite lines on Canvas 2-D: double, tapered, overshooting, boiling 8× a second; paper-white knock-outs; graphite tone (hair, pupils) — **no colour** |
| lens | shallow depth of field (f/2.8 at a few centimetres to a metre), warm late-day grade, grain | blurred by the *same* lens when it is out of focus; under the same grain and vignette |
| light | the HDRI's own light; real shadows and contact shadows | casts a real shadow and sits in a soft contact shadow on whatever it stands on |

## The grammar (what the clip does, in order)
1. **The page in the world.** Close on the drawn figure standing on a sketchbook page lying on a real table; the real place
   is a soft blur behind. The figure is alive at once (looks around, breathes).
2. **The real touches the drawn.** A real thing comes into the drawing's world — in the clip a finger pokes the page and
   the girl reacts. Here: the real pencil arrives; the hero startles (anticipation, hop, settle).
3. **The real pencil draws a way out.** The pencil — held by someone just out of frame — draws something that **stands up in
   3-D** from the page (a door in the clip). Its lines are drawn on stroke by stroke and the camera's parallax proves they
   stand in space.
4. **Off the page.** The hero walks off the paper onto the real table: a drawing on real wood, with a real shadow.
5. **Down into the world.** A drawn way down (stairs in the clip) from the table to the ground; the camera cranes down with
   the hero, from table height to the hero's own eye height on the ground. The real place grows huge.
6. **The real world, at the hero's scale.** A low camera on the real ground (cobbles sharp at the feet, the street a blur);
   the hero looks around in awe.
7. **The drawn becomes real.** A drawn thing on the ground (a daisy in the clip) turns real when the hero touches it — the
   one moment colour appears on a drawn thing. A drawn companion (a butterfly) flutters up.
8. **The pencil closes the frame.** The real pencil comes back and draws a shape around the hero (a heart); outside it the
   world turns back to white paper. End.

## What this skill fixes and what it leaves to the topic
- One take: the camera flows (no cuts) from the page down to the ground; scale changes are camera moves.
- The drawing is graphite only. Colour belongs to the real world. The only "drawn thing in colour" is a drawing that has
  **become** a real object (a scan or a built mesh), revealed from the touch point.
- The real pencil appears only where its far end can stay out of frame (close shots); in wide frames lines draw themselves.
- The ending returns the world to paper inside a drawn frame.
- Per topic: the hero, the place (HDRI + surfaces), the way out and the way down (door, slide, ladder, umbrella, bridge,
  rope, paper plane …), what turns real (any scan or built object), what the closing frame is.

## Small things that sell it
- The real pencil's hexagon catches the light and casts a shadow on the page; it never floats (its back end leaves frame).
- Drawn lines boil, but the figure moves smoothly; a drawn thing standing in 3-D keeps its perspective as the camera moves.
- The drawn paper-white matches the real page's lit colour (one calibration render), so the figure looks like the same paper.
- The contact shadow under the feet does more for "it stands there" than the cast shadow.
- Depth of field on everything, including the drawing; grain over both worlds together.
