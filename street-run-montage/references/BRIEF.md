# BRIEF — topic → a ride, and the reviews

Write `BRIEF.md` in the project before any code (`templates/BRIEF.md` is the form). The brief turns a topic into a ride:
who rides, who runs with them, where they are going and why, what happens on the way, and which camera sees each beat.

## 1. The topic as a ride
Every topic becomes **a journey with a goal**: someone has to get somewhere (the last ferry, a friend's window before
sunset, the first day of the holidays, a lost cap carried back) and the companion comes along. The goal is the last
shot (the destination in frame, the stop, the cheer). If the topic is a feeling or a theme ("freedom", "summer"),
invent the errand that carries it.

## 2. The cast (cast.js)
| field | decide |
|---|---|
| rider | age and build (`height`, `build`), **mass** (sets crouch depth and timing), stance (`regular` / `goofy`), skin, hair (`long`, `bob`, `short`) and colour, eyes, cap (colour, a short logo you invent) or beanie, top (oversized hoodie colour, hood lining, a short print), bottom, socks, shoes (colour + accent), board (deck, grip, wheels, a graphic in 3 colours) |
| signature | one thing that reads at 30 px tall: a colour block (mint hoodie, red beanie), the hair silhouette, the cap |
| idle | how they stand when the ride is over (weight on one leg, hands in the pocket, breathing) |
| companion | `kind` (cat, dog, corgi, fox, or a new kind), coat / belly / socks / tail tip, eyes, ears, collar, **mass**, temperament (keeps ahead, lags then sprints, leaps walls, barks at tricks) |

Never the source clip's skater or black cat, never the template's kid and dog or the example's girl and corgi unless
the topic is exactly theirs. Never a real brand on a cap, a print or a sign.

## 3. Place, season, light
Season (`autumn` · `spring` · `summer` · `winter`) and light (`noon` · `afternoon` · `golden`) follow the topic.
The route is 4–6 places in order (a back lane down a hill, a shopping street, a riverside path, a park edge, a station
plaza, a harbour), each a zone or built from pieces, with 1–2 landmarks the shots can use (a planter to ollie, a ledge
to grind, a bridge in the distance, the destination building). Write every sign's text (invented, in the topic's spirit).

## 4. Beats (story time)
6–10 moments, each a line: **time · move · where · gather → action → settle · what the companion does · sound**.
Speed keys rise only with a push or a slope. Put the big tricks where the music lifts. End with the slow-down, the step
off, the idle, the cheer (and the companion's sit and call).

## 5. Shots (film time, beats)
One camera per beat (references/CAMERA.md): what it looks at, why this camera, its length in beats, slow motion or not.
1.5–3 s average, ≥ 5 kinds, never the same camera twice in a row. Open wide (the place), close on the feet early, give
the companion its own shot, save the worm's-eye for the highest jump, end on a still `hero` shot of the destination.

## 6. Tune
Key, mode, bpm (110–130), an 8-bar tune in scale degrees, the chord roots, which shot is the drop, the beds per place
(birds, city, river), one or two placed effects (a horn, a crossing bell).

## Cast review (score the cast sheet, 0–10 each; fix anything under 7)
1. **One body, one direction**: face, chest and toes point the same way in every cell; the knees bend forward; riding
   side-on the head turns toward the nose (left foot forward = regular).
2. **Both sides**: right and left rows read as the same person; the cap logo, print and hair are not mirrored wrong.
3. **Board**: under both feet in ride, gather, land; under and turning in the flip; feet just above it, not floating.
4. **Moves**: the gather is lower than the ride; the air pose tucks with arms out for balance (not a T); the landing
   absorbs lower than the gather; the cheer reaches up from a dip.
5. **Companion**: paws on the ground in the gaits, front lower legs fold back, hind feet fold forward, the tail trails
   in the gallop, the sit has both hind paws forward and the chest up; the face reads from the front.
6. **Silhouette**: the rider's signature and the companion's kind read at thumbnail size.

## Still review (a still per shot; 0–10 each; fix anything under 7)
1. **Subject**: what the shot is about is in frame, big enough, not cut at the head or the board.
2. **Camera**: not inside a wall, not under the ground, not staring at a hedge; the line to the subject is clear.
3. **Speed**: on moving shots the background streaks and the bodies stay sharp; the streak follows the motion.
4. **Weight**: nothing floats, slides or stands dead; the trick in this shot gathers and lands; the companion's paws
   are planted when they should be.
5. **Place**: the zone reads (lane, shops, river, harbour …); signs are legible where they matter; the destination is
   in the last shot.
6. **Look**: two tones only, cool shadows, warm skin, clean ink on bodies, no grey "3D default" surfaces.
"Is the neck turned or the body?", "is that foot skating?", "where are they?" — any such question = fail.
