## The look (observed)

- **The world is archive engraving**: black line work on white — parallel lines swelling with the tone, cross-hatching
  in deep shadow, line-screen skies; no colour anywhere in the world.
- **Cut-outs as 3-D layers**: each subject is a separate card, cut close to the drawing, standing at an angle in 3-D;
  soft grey drop shadows behind the cards (paper lifted off paper); far cards paler; near ones out of focus.
- **Cards that move**: buildings rotate or hinge into place as the camera passes; panoramas bend; waves roll (puppet pins).
- **Characters**: cel-animated children in flat colour — one base, one shade, thin dark lines, no texture — the only
  colour in the frame.
- **Camera**: big, smooth 3-D moves — fly-throughs, push-ins, trucks along streets, cranes past towers.
- **The breakdown**: a working view from outside the shot camera; checkerboard, layer-coloured outlines and handles,
  the frustum and the motion path; the same scene, not a separate drawing.

## What this skill takes

- The **technique**: subjects engraved, cut out and staged as 3-D cards; line-screen skies; paper-lift shadows; depth
  haze and focus; cards that rise, drift and sway; cel characters as the only colour; sweeping cameras.
- The **format**: the comp on top and the 3-D breakdown underneath (and the comp alone).

## What it does not take

- *Lucy Lost*'s characters (the girl in the red dress and bow, the white-haired boy in orange overalls), its story, its
  New York locations and landmarks, its shots, its archive engravings, its character animation.
- Scanned engravings: here every plate is drawn by the engraver in code (`lib/engrave.js`), so a piece can engrave any
  subject its topic needs and stays free of anyone else's images.
- Hand-drawn frame-by-frame character animation: characters here are a cel-painted rig posed by key tables.
