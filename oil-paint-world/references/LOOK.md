# LOOK — what makes it read as oil paint

Observed from the source post's 16 s clip (a cloaked wanderer running through an autumn wood: stills at 1 s intervals) and
from the Oil paint project's own description of itself. These are the style; a topic changes none of them.

1. **Everything is a stroke, and strokes have edges.** Leaves are single pressed dabs; trunks are long vertical drags
   that run dry; the ground is a carpet of short strokes lying every way; grass is single upward flicks. There are no
   smooth surfaces and no lines. Where a stroke ends you see the next one under it.
2. **Paint is pinned to the world.** When the eye moves, strokes move with their things. Nothing swims, nothing is
   redrawn in screen space. (The ground's dabs are real patches of ground; far off, one in four is drawn larger to stand
   for its neighbours, and it shrinks back where it lies as you come near.)
3. **Three mixes, broken.** Each thing is painted from three colours mixed in advance — light, half-tone, shade — and
   each dab takes one according to how lit its spot is, then is nudged in value, hue and strength. So a sunlit bank is
   not a gradient but a field of slightly different yellows, and a shadow's edge is a ragged line of whole dabs.
4. **Shade is a colour.** Shadows in the clip are plum and wine on a gold floor; trunks go to blue-violet; the cloak's
   shade is ultramarine. Nothing goes to black, and lights stop short of white.
5. **The sun is ahead.** Most of the clip looks toward a low sun: trunks are dark against lit mist, leaves glow where the
   light comes through them, shadows fall toward the eye, and the air between the trunks is lit in shafts.
6. **Mist makes depth.** Each row of trunks is paler and warmer (toward the sun) or greyer (away) than the one before.
   Far things are fewer, larger touches.
7. **The figure is paint too.** A few hundred strokes: the cloak a mass of long strokes following its fall, legs two dark
   drags. Its shadow is the one soft thing in the picture — a glaze that slides over the ground's strokes.
8. **Relief, weave, varnish.** Each stroke stands up a little and is lit from the upper left, so strokes have a bright
   edge and a dark edge whatever is painted; under thin paint the linen shows; darks are a touch deeper, as if varnished.
9. **The frame.** Third person, the wanderer about a third of the frame's height, the eye a little above their head and
   behind; low grass sometimes crosses the lens; the eye sometimes looks up into a crown.

What is *not* in the look: outlines, cel bands, bloom, lens flares, depth of field, film grain, motion blur, text.
