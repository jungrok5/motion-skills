# APERTURE — brief

**Topic given:** "a camera lens". Not in the reference film (a watch escapement, a car, a clock temple).

## One line
A lens seen from the inside out: the iris opens, the scales turn, fifteen parts become one lens, the lens meets its
camera, the camera takes one photograph — and that photograph, and the camera, turn out to be lying on the maker's desk.

## Design decided for this topic
- **Subject and mechanism:** a 50 mm f/1.4 lens: nine-blade iris (real pivot geometry, checked for light leaks when stopped
  down), six coated glass elements, brass helicoid, three engraved rings; a 35 mm rangefinder body.
- **Palette:** tungsten amber (2700-3000 K practicals) against steel blue (6500-9000 K fill), black set. Brass and red
  paint are the only saturated materials. The coast inverts it: warm everywhere, cool only in the water's shadows.
- **Materials:** blued steel blades (brushed), turned brass, black anodising with engraved white / orange / red paint,
  satin chrome, coated glass (each element a different coating thickness → magenta, green, amber reflections),
  pebbled leather, lacquer; oak, leather mat, photo paper on the desk.
- **Light:** macro: one cool raking strip, one warm kick, a hot source seen through the hole. Studio: warm strips *in*
  the picture, two long boxes for the highlights that draw the body. Coast: physical sky, sun 3.2° up. Desk: one
  tungsten lamp, a blue window.
- **Lens and camera moves:** macro 100 mm f/9-16; studio 65-90 mm f/4-5.6; coast 38 mm f/8; desk 50 mm f/4; dive 28→20 mm.
  One move per shot: push, crane up the barrel, arc, drift, lift, rush.
- **Title:** APERTURE, wide-tracked brass capitals with a rule, in front of the iris.
- **Sound:** D drone; ticks for every detent; a clunk for every part that seats; shutter on the two white cuts; surf and a
  held D-minor chord on the coast; a riser through the dive; D major on the title.
- **Length:** 432 frames = 18.0 s.
