# FEELINGS — turning a story into a picture that contains none of it

The source prompt's hardest rule: evoke the story's emotions, but draw nothing from it. This is how.

## 1. Inventory, not summary

Split the story into beats (one row each). For every beat write the feeling under it, not the event:
*the investor says nothing* → **suspense, being weighed**; *pizza guy drew an elephant on the lid* → **being known,
small kindness**. Give each a weight (1–5), a temperature (cold / cool / warm / hot), a motion (none, sinking, rising,
swelling, scattering, spinning), and a light (dim, flat, one point, glow, flood, sparkle). Then pick the top 2–3 feelings —
the ones a stranger should name. Everything else supports them.

## 2. Never drawn

List every noun in the story: people, objects, places, brands, food, devices, words, symbols, numbers. Also their obvious
stand-ins (a story about a check → no paper slips with numbers; a story about a game console → no controllers, no pixel
characters, no screens). `check.mjs` refuses these words as names in the code; the rubric refuses them in the picture.

## 3. Translate each feeling into something that can be seen

Carry feelings with things the story does not contain. A starting vocabulary (combine, invert, invent):

Rules of thumb:
- **One place, one time, one light.** All the feelings must live in one picture; if two feelings fight, let the sky hold
  one and the land hold the other (cold land, warm sky; dark ceiling, gold tear).
- **The strongest feeling gets the focus** (lightest light next to darkest dark), the second gets the second place, the
  rest become texture (a crowd of small things, the direction of marks, the temperature of shadows).
- **A crowd of small things** carries "many voices, many people, many moments" without drawing a person: kites, birds,
  lanterns, windows, fireflies, petals, sparks. Pick one the story does not mention.
- **Motion lives in the marks**: radiating strokes for a burst, diagonals for speed, horizontals for calm, spirals for
  wonder.
- **Two stories** (the source had two) → two layers of the same picture: one in the sky, one in the land, or one in the
  light and one in the colour. Never two pictures side by side.

## 4. Check the translation before drawing

Read the translation table without the story. Could someone guess a beat (a check, a karaoke room, a console)? Then it is
too literal — go one step further away (a check → *a burst of light*, not *a paper slip*; karaoke → *many warm lights
together*, not *a microphone*).
