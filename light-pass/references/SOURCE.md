  A 15 s before/after of an After Effects shot: a flat, overcast forest turned into a sunlit one by drawing a masked path for
  every light beam between the trees, shaping each beam's curve and spread, adding elliptical masks for glow, and grading.
  The post's point: a "simple" sunlit shot is many hand-placed masks, with the light's direction, brightness and shadow
  designed piece by piece.

What light-pass takes from it: the method — each beam a shape placed by hand from where the light enters to where it lands,
pools as soft ellipses, the grade last — and the mask view for placing them. What it adds: the shapes are numbers in a plan
(so they keyframe and check), and a scattered-ray pass that follows the picture's real gaps, which a static mask cannot do
when the picture moves. No footage, frame or asset from the post is used; the example frames are stills from the
`machine-wilds` film, drawn by its own engine.
