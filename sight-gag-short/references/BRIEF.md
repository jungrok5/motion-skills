# BRIEF — fixed vs per topic, the template, the stills rubric

Write the brief into the project as `BRIEF.md` **before any code**. The format is fixed; everything a topic changes is
designed fresh in the brief. The worked example (`examples/crossing-guard/BRIEF.md`) and the template
(`templates/BRIEF.md`) are filled-in examples of this page.

## What is fixed (the style)
- 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 25–45 s, **no dialogue**, no captions until the pun title.
- **One hero**, deadpan, who strolls (never runs) through **one place** and acts only with small looks; the hero smiles
  once, on the last beat, and may glance at the camera on a button.
- **One gadget** that comes out of a machine on the hero's back and does all the acting: stretches, flicks, grabs,
  scolds, gives a thumbs up. Default: a giant inflated glove on a rubber hose.
- **4–7 gags**, one locked wide shot each, 3.5–5 s (8–10 beats at 120 bpm), cut on the beat; every gag is
  **setup → notice → act → result → button**; help and comeuppance alternate and each gag is a notch bigger.
- **Look:** a sunny painted-3D street — warm sun from the upper left, blue-violet shadows, painted brick with flaked
  plaster and chips, white-painted brick, saturated shopfronts and striped awnings, vinyl-toy people.
- **Sound:** one continuous groove from the first frame to the title, cartoon foley on every contact.
- **End:** a pun title over the hero's last beat.

## What is designed per topic
hero (job, build, outfit, face, hair/hat, the machine) · gadget (style, colour, hose, or another tip) · place
(buildings, signs, props, time of day stays sunny) · the cast of nuisances and helped people · every gag and its
physical joke · the pun · the groove (key, tempo 110–130, sections, stops).

## Stills rubric (score each 0–2 at every `act` and `button` mark; fix anything under 2)
1. **The gag reads with the sound off** in one still at its `act` mark — who did what to whom is obvious.
2. **The gadget is the actor:** its shape and pose say the intention (stop, pinch, flick, grab, scold, thumbs up).
3. **The hero is deadpan and in frame;** the gadget never fully leaves frame mid-gag and isn't cut by the frame top.
4. **The hose is one continuous stretch** from the machine to the cuff, no kinks; it sags when slack, trails on whips.
5. **Grounded:** contact shadows, no feet under the pavement, held props stay in hands, caught things stay caught.
6. **The look:** bright sun from the left, blue-violet shadows, painted brick and chips, saturated shopfronts, sky
   visible above lower buildings; nothing grey or flat.
7. **Faces read at film size:** eyes, lids, brows and mouth say the emotion (smug → shock → sheepish).
8. **Nothing unintended:** no lone raised finger palm-on (reads as the middle finger), no brand names, no real people,
   no text but signs and the title.
9. **Rhythm:** the act lands within a beat of the cut's middle; the button leaves ~0.8 s before the cut.
10. **Escalation:** each gag is bigger than the last; the last gag is the biggest stretch.
