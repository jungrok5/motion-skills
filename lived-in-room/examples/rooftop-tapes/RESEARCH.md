## shell — the room's box
- what: a rooftop room (옥탑방) added on a 1990s four-storey brick-faced walk-up in Seoul.
- numbers (opened): a habitable room's ceiling is ≥ 2.1 m under the building rules and ≥ 2.2 m (storey ≥ 2.4 m) under
  the housing rule, dimensions in 5 cm steps; a rooftop structure is not a storey while it is ≤ 1/8 of the building
  footprint — which is why lived-in ones are tiny or unpermitted additions; a masonry party wall is ≥ 200 mm with its finish.
- numbers (estimate): concrete block 390 × 190 × 150 with plaster both faces ≈ 190 mm; real ceilings 2.2–2.3 m; floor area 10–20 m².
- used here: 3.3 × 4.3 m inside (14.2 m²), ceiling 2.25 m, walls 190 mm.
- whose way of doing it: the builder who added it — block, not panel; the lowest ceiling the rule allows.
- source: https://www.midascad.com/cad_archive/buildingact-2 · https://ko.wikisource.org/wiki/주택건설기준_등에_관한_규칙 ·

- numbers (opened, maker's catalogue): frame depth — inner single 120 mm, inner double 232 mm, balcony double 250 mm;
  glazing 22 / 24 / 26 mm; crescent lock 80, 81 or 105 mm long; sash pull handles 278–298 mm. On a third storey or higher a
  window whose sill is under 1,100 mm needs a 1,200 mm guard rail (housing rule, art. 18).

  a 70 mm reveal inside under a stool board; 1500 × 1200, sill 900 — it opens onto the roof terrace, not onto a drop, so

## beacon — the red lights on the skyline
- what: aviation obstruction lights, 국토교통부 고시 제2024-39호 「항공장애물 관리 및 비행안전 확인 기준」, chapter 4 and table 9.
- numbers (opened): medium-intensity type B is red and **flashes 20–60 times a minute**; low-intensity types A / B and
  medium type C are **steady** red. Ordinary buildings need lights from 150 m; chimneys, towers, masts and lattice structures
  from 60 m (art. 30). A structure of 45–150 m lit with type B alternates flashing type B and steady low-intensity lights
  on its levels (art. 33).
- used here: nothing on the four-storey buildings (none is required); a mast and a chimney on the two hills each carry a
  flashing top light (30 and 46 a minute — different rates, so they drift in and out of step) and a steady light half-way
  up. `ROOM['beacon_range'] = (20, 60)`; `r.beacon()` refuses a rate outside it. The neon crosses are signs: steady.
- source: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?flSeq=136894377

## spine — how a Korean book's spine is set
- numbers (opened): in writing systems with a vertical tradition the spine title runs top to bottom, as the language does.
  (estimate): Hangul is stacked upright; Latin words and numerals in the same spine are turned a quarter clockwise; title,
  then author, then the publisher's mark at the foot.
- used here: `lr.draw.spine()` does exactly that; the tape spines are a hand-written line turned to read top to bottom.

## handheld — the phone in the visitor's hand
- numbers (opened): adults walk at about 109 ± 13 steps a minute with steps of 0.57–0.66 m; a hand's physiological tremor
  is 8–12 Hz; at 24 frames a second with a 180° shutter, "pan no faster than a full image width every seven seconds";
  a current phone's main camera is 26 mm-equivalent at ƒ/1.6, the phone 147.6 × 71.6 × 7.8 mm.
- derived: a 26 mm lens sees 69° across, so a full width in seven seconds is about 10° a second.
- used here: `r.walk(step=0.65, pan_cap=12)`: the picture rises and falls once a step and only while walking; the tremor has
  a slow part and a small 9–10 Hz part; the fastest turn in either walk is under 9° a second. Lens 24 mm, depth of field
  as ƒ/6 on full frame (a small sensor at ƒ/1.6).
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104090 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological_tremor ·
  https://www.reddigitalcinema.com/red-101/camera-panning-speed · https://www.apple.com/iphone-16/specs/
