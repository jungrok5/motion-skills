# SOURCE — what this skill was made from

**The post:** Vamsi Batchu (@vamsibatchuk) on X —
https://x.com/vamsibatchuk/status/2121788887823778297?a=NmhF1v14IKYs7TtTVf4&s=19 — answering people who asked for the
prompt: he gave a reference illustration by **@Artedeingenio** ("check their work") to a model and asked it to make
artworks on many different subjects **in pure JavaScript, p5.js or Python code, with no outside files and no skills**,
adding that the approach works with almost any style. The post shows the reference: a girl drawn in the
contemporary picture-book manner — matte gouache skin, a dark dry-brushed bob with blue streaks and flyaway hairs, big
round eyes with small pupils glancing aside, a pink nose, a tiny frown, red cheeks and ears, a long neck, a striped
knit cardigan in crayon texture, on white. (Read from the screenshot the owner shared, 2026-11-16.)

## What this skill takes

- the **method**: a reference style → code that draws new subjects in it, with no image files at all;
- the **style grammar**, as rules and tools: gouache skin, dry-brush hair with coloured streaks, crayon clothes with
  paper tooth, thin wobbling ink contours that overshoot and break, flyaway hairs, big round side-glancing eyes, small
  features low on a big head, strong blush, long neck, cropped bust, a white sheet around;
- "works with any style" read the other way: **any subject** in this one style — the brief designs a new character for
  each topic.

## What it does not take

- the reference illustration or its character: no long black-and-blue bob with a striped yellow-and-violet cardigan,
  no copy of that face. The defaults in `lib/figure.js` are deliberately different (a plain coral sweater, teal streaks),
  and the starter and examples are this repo's own characters (a boy in the rain, a beekeeper, a baker);
- any image file, font or outside asset: everything is drawn by code on a canvas;
- any claim about the artist's process: the media here are simulated from what the picture shows.

@Artedeingenio's work is the original of the look this skill learned from; credit them when a piece made with it is
shared, the way the post did.
