# living-realm

A place you walk through, in the look of a current game engine, that is **one HTML file**: three.js r170 inlined as a
thin WebGL 2 layer, no images, models, fonts, HDRIs or sound files, no network. Materials, geometry, plants, sky and
sound are computed when the page opens. A realm is a folder of small source files (the land, the town, the details,
what grows, the words, the start); `build.mjs` joins them with the engine into `index.html`. The page opens on a
slow tour through the place while the hour and the weather go round; the first key or touch hands you the walk.

Made from the post by Rez (@RezAIT00) on X — <https://x.com/RezAIT00/status/2120498246426706176?a=CUYTAAEWhBIL6PS4ZPk&s=19>
("THIS IS NOT A VIDEO MODEL. This is fable 5.2 with blender and unreal … create me some game assets but to really
focus on the environment"): a 48-second clip of an Unreal Engine forest and meadow — sun shafts through a canopy,
back-lit ferns, a wind-combed meadow with orange flowers, mossy boulders, an old oak. The post published no code and no
assets, so **nothing of it is here**: the look was read off its frames (`references/SOURCE.md`, `references/LOOK.md`)
and this engine was written for the browser to carry the same ingredients — and what the request asked for on top of
it: a town, the hour, rain, winter, walking, and speed. A second reference was given later to raise the look —
「Larkhollow」 by @lucian__03, a farm generated from code, <https://claude.ai/artifact/D8t6NHbpDJXtCovZU6KADP> — and
was rendered side by side with the village and measured (key, contrast, colour, how dark the shade is, how clear the
air): the tone, the dark-rooted grass, the worn walls and the contact shadows answer it; none of its code is here.
A third, 「Saltline」 (a port generated from code, <https://claude.ai/artifact/BGY1SztrAYcJQHSsnHHGmp>), and a
picture of the look wanted (wet paving that mirrors, rays from the sun, a sky full of stars) came after: the mirror
on wet ground, the shafts and the Milky Way answer those.
Built and proven in this repo on `examples/village`
「은여울 마을」 (Silverford: a square with a fountain, an inn, a smithy with a lit forge, a chapel on a knoll, a mill on
the brook, a windmill, a stone bridge to a flower meadow, a palisade gate, a farm, a pond that freezes; 300 m across,
woods and mountains beyond). `starter/` (Tarn Cabin: a forester's cabin by a mountain lake) is a second, unrelated topic.

**Fixed by the skill (the style)**
- One file, zero assets, zero network; WebGL 2; everything generated at load; sound synthesised.
- The frame: G-buffer → cascaded sun/moon shadows → ambient occlusion → fog marched through the shadows → a bounce of
  light off what is on screen → one lighting pass (sun with contact shadows, computed sky, the bounce, lamps) → water and particles → temporal resolve that upsamples → bloom → eye
  adaptation → an AgX display transform and a grade that follows the hour (contrast, colour, key and the sky's fill
  are four numbers on the weather state). Every surface is a baked height + colour + roughness material.
- One state for the hour, the season and the weather that everything reads; nothing switches — clouds gather before
  rain, stone darkens before puddles fill, leaves turn before snow lies. Rain and snow reach only what is open to the sky.
- A world in metres you walk at eye height: W A S D / arrows, Q E or ← → to turn, mouse or touch to look, Shift to run;
  solids, decks and deep water are respected; a tour until you take over; named places announce themselves.
- A HUD with the hour, the weather, an automatic round, quality and sound; four quality tiers and a governor that
  trades internal resolution for frame rate. `check.mjs` must pass.

**Designed for every topic (never reused from the example or the starter)**
- The place: whose it is, where and when; why it stands where it does; its plan, landmarks, ways, water and edges.
- Its building tradition: walls, roofs, openings, ornament — as new builders made from the kit's parts, and new
  materials where the stock ones are wrong.
- Its land (the height function, water, cover maps), its trees and ground cover (species from numbers), its small things.
- What each state shows here: what is lit at night, where puddles gather, what the snow lies on, what freezes.
- The walk (spawn, tour, views), the sounds placed in it, the words on the glass and their language.

## Project layout (what you create)
```
my-realm/
  BRIEF.md            written first (references/BRIEF.md)
  realm.json          { "title", "subtitle", "lang", "bg" }
  src/10_land.js      the height function, water, ways, cover maps; a shared sheet of coordinates
  src/15_words.js     place names and the HUD's strings (any language)       (optional)
  src/20_town.js      buildings — the kit's, and builders of your own from its parts
  src/30_details.js   lamps, stalls, signs, fences, rocks: what makes it lived in
  src/40_plants.js    species of your own, named trees, scatter by a woods function, the far forest, flowers
  src/90_main.js      LR.start({ build, weather, tour, views, strings })        ← always last
  index.html          built (the realm)
  out/                stills, sheets, film.mp4
```
Sources are plain scripts joined in name order into one module: a later file sees the earlier ones; no import/export.
A `const` at the top level of one file is in scope in every later file.

## Requirements
Node 18+ with Playwright Chromium, ffmpeg (sheets and films). No npm install, no keys. The page itself needs WebGL 2
and wants a real GPU; without one (CI, containers) stills render on the CPU at 10–60 s each.

## Workflow
1. **Topic → brief.** Read `references/BRIEF.md` and write `BRIEF.md` in the project: the place in one line, its
   setting (building tradition, ground, plants, water, light, sounds), the plan with coordinates (the middle, 5–9
   landmarks, ways, water, edges, far country), what each state shows here, what must be made new, the walk, the words.
   Research what you do not know. The village example's houses belong to its brief.
2. **Start the folder.** `node living-realm/scripts/new-realm.mjs my-realm` and write `src/10_land.js`: the height
   function, a `woods(x, z)` function, water, roads and squares, cover maps — then `LR.land.roughen()` and patches of
   rank grass, worn earth and flowers painted by noise: nothing people use is level or even (`references/LOOK.md`).
3. **Land and light first.** With no buildings yet, render `--shot` stills from three or four spots at noon, at dusk
   and from 200 m up looking down (`cam=0,0,0,-89.9,230`): is the plan there, do ways and water read, is the skyline good?
4. **Buildings.** Use `LR.arch.house` and the builders in `42_buildings.js` where they are right for the topic; where
   they are not, write a builder in the project from the parts (`A.wall`, `A.window`, `A.door`, `A.gableRoof`, `LR.Geo`)
   and define the materials it needs with `LR.mat.define` (`references/KIT.md`). Check each new building alone in a
   still before placing twenty. Look at `?view=mats` after adding materials.
5. **Details and plants.** `A.dress(house)` for every house (bare wall feet are what makes a place look unbuilt), then
   props where people would leave them; lamps where people walk at night; named trees, then
   scatter by the woods function with an `avoid` that keeps trunks off ways, water and walls — with a density that
   varies, so the canopy has gaps for the sun; flowers and ferns on the cover maps.
6. **The walk.** Spawn, tour way-points (each with what that stretch looks at), 8–12 views, named places, sounds.
   Then `check.mjs my-realm --walk` (one page load): it lists every stretch of the tour that is inside something and
   draws `out/walk.png` — solids, water, ways, the tour and the views from above. Read the map: a fence across a
   road or a house on a path is a fault in the plan, not in the tour. Then `render.mjs my-realm --tour 12 --scale .5`:
   twelve stills along the tour as its camera looks — the page opens on this walk, so no stretch of it may stare at
   a blank wall, a hedge or the ground.
7. **Judge it against what was asked for, then verify.** Put the stills beside the reference (or the brief's own
   words) and list where they fall short, worst first — air and light before plants before surfaces — fix the top of
   the list, render again, repeat (`references/LESSONS.md` has the list this skill's own example went through).
   `node living-realm/scripts/check.mjs my-realm` must print `check OK` (it walks the whole tour against
   every solid and the water, and renders views in day, dusk, night, rain and snow). Then `render.mjs --sheet` and score
   it with the review in `references/BRIEF.md`; then stand at the spawn point and render four stills 90° apart. Fix and
   repeat. Open `index.html` in a browser with a GPU and walk it; press `P` for the numbers.
8. **Deliver.** `index.html` is the realm (it runs from disk or any static host). Optionally `render.mjs --film` for
   an MP4 of the tour with the automatic round (on a machine with a GPU: `--gpu`).

## Rules
- **Design for the topic.** The example's timber houses, clay tiles, beech woods, lane and square belong to its brief.
  A new topic gets its own plan, builders, materials, species and words; reuse the kit's parts, not the designs.
- **One style.** Whatever you add obeys the same laws: generated at load, a baked material with relief, written into
  the G-buffer through the shared weather (`applyWeather`), lit by the one lighting pass, in metres.
- **No assets, no network, one file.** No loaders, no `data:` URIs, no web fonts.
- **Everything opaque.** Leaves, grass and fences are alpha-tested with a dithered threshold, never blended; only water
  and particles are see-through, and they are drawn after the lit world by the kits that own them.
- **Weather goes through the state.** Never set fog, sun colour or wetness in a realm; change `LR.wx.looks` or the
  state. Anything that should get wet or snowed on writes the G-buffer through `applyWeather`; anything under a roof
  stays dry because the roof is in the rain map (built things are added with `LR.world.add`).
- **Openings are real.** Windows and doors are recesses in walls with frames; do not paint them on.
- **The walker must be able to walk.** Every building, prop and trunk registers its solid; bridges and piers register
  decks; the tour and every view stand on open ground. `check.mjs` enforces it.
- **Budget** (`references/PERF.md`): one mesh per building, props through `LR.props.g`, trees through `LR.flora`,
  ground cover through the cover maps — never thousands of separate meshes. Keep a view under ~520 draw calls and
  ~4 M triangles on `medium`.
- **Say where the look stops** (`references/LOOK.md`): generated geometry, reflections of what is on screen only, no characters.
- **Never put `out/` or an MP4 in a repository.**

## Contract
- `LR.start({ build(LR) (may be async), weather: { tod, weather }, tour: [{ x, z, look: [x, z], pull, pitch }], views:
  [{ name, cam: [x, z, compass yaw°, pitch°, y?] }], strings, materials(LR) })`; `window.READY` when the page can draw.
- Page options: `?q=low|medium|high|ultra`, `?scale=`, `?mat=512`, `?off=ao,gi,skyshare,shadow,contact,mirror,shafts,bloom,taa,grass,fx,water,ssr,audio`,
  `?tod=`, `?wx=clear|rain|snow|winter|autumn`, `?wet=1&puddle=1` (just after rain), `?tour=0`, `?auto=0`, `?at=x,z,yaw`, `?stats=1`, `?hud=0`, `?view=mats`.
- Still mode `?render=1&w=&h=`: `window.realm = { canvas, shot({ cam, tod, weather, season, snow, wet, frames, t }) →
  info, info(), place(), filmStart(fps), filmFrame(), LR }`. `info()` reports tier, sizes, draw calls, triangles, lamps.
- Hooks: `LR.on('built' | 'init' | 'input' | 'frame' | 'after' | 'key' | 'thunder' | 'tour', fn, order)`.
- A material: `LR.mat.define(name, { glsl: 'void surf(vec2 uv, out vec3 col, out float h, out float rough, out float
  metal)', tile, bump, por, pud, stick, moss, metal, pom, paint })` — `uv` 0..1 and it **must tile**.
- A built thing: write an `LR.Geo`, `LR.world.add(geo)`, register `LR.world.solidBox / solidCircle`, lights with
  `LR.world.light`, smoke with `LR.world.smokers.push`, sounds with `LR.world.sounds.push`.
