# Message check — answer before you show a storyboard

A beautiful frame is not the same as "this is about me". The first "door" short (word-log, 2026-10) looked frightening
but carried half its message: the door opened by itself (no "me"), three metaphors were mixed (door, leaven, Passover),
and the turn (blood on the doorposts) was unreadable to anyone outside church. Version 2 fixed it with these questions.
Fill them in for every storyboard (12 questions); if one can't be answered, change the scene, not the captions.

1. **Is the viewer in the picture?** First-person hand, room, choice — or a figure who is plainly "me". Things that
   happen by themselves are someone else's story.
2. **Who acts on each beat** (me / the threat / grace)? Is the agent of the turn visible?
3. **One metaphor.** Do the image and the on-screen quotes use the same one? Move other metaphors to the description.
4. **≤ 2 quotes on screen**, each locked to the image.
5. **Outsider test.** Can someone who doesn't know the source read the turn from the picture alone?
6. **Picture-only test.** Sound off, captions covered: does the arc (start → build → decisive moment → turn) still read?
7. **Emotion curve.** One word per beat (curiosity → unease → dread → realisation → relief). Cut beats that don't make it.
8. **A line to remember** at the peak, in plain words of your own (e.g. "열어 준 건 나였다 — I was the one who opened it").
9. **Intensity** fits an all-ages audience; evil by suggestion (shadow, hand), no gore.
10. **Interpretation test.** Is there no moment where a first-time viewer thinks "what does this mean?" Too short or too
    compressed is as fatal as too long — unpack symbols and ellipses until the picture is direct.
11. **Curiosity chain.** Does every beat raise "so what happens next?" and does the next beat answer it? Viewers leave
    where the chain breaks. (A shortened cut of the same film was tried and dropped: trimming alone leaves it half-made.)
12. **Last frame ≈ first frame**, so a Short replays seamlessly.
