# BRIEF — from a topic to a film plan

Every film starts as `BRIEF.md` in the project, written before any code (`templates/BRIEF.md` is the form). The brief is
where the topic turns into *this* film: the skill fixes the grammar, the brief designs everything that grammar carries.

## Fixed vs designed per topic
| fixed by the skill (the style) | designed per topic (in the brief) |
|---|---|
| 16:9, 30 fps, 50–120 s | the thesis: one sentence the film argues |
| 3–6 numbered chapters + the closing card | the chapters: one idea each, a 1–4 word title + an English name |
| chrome: badge, brand mark, typed subtitle + English line, callouts, seal card | the lines (native + English), the accent words, the brand mark, the seal character(s), the card's title and line |
| slow camera paths, cross-fade within a chapter, dip through paper between chapters | which places (sets) and which objects carry each idea; where the camera goes |
| light presets (`paper · garden · interior · golden · hall · night · snow`) | which preset each shot uses; colours of materials inside it (wood, glaze, tile, plaster) |
| far things dissolve into paper / fog; procedural geometry and canvas textures only | new pieces the topic needs, built in the project from kit pieces |
| a score of plucked strings, flute, drone, bells; one scale | the scale and tonic, the mood per chapter, the knocks and bells |

The template and the worked example are examples of the style. A topic gets the template's lantern or the example's
moon jar only if it is about lanterns or moon jars.

## Turning a topic into chapters
1. **Thesis.** One sentence, usually "X's beauty is the beauty of Y" (Korean beauty → what is left unfilled; wabi-sabi
   → what time has worn; Gothic → light made into structure). Every chapter is evidence for it.
2. **Chapters.** Pick 3–6 things a viewer can *see* that carry the thesis — one painting habit, one building element,
   one material, one object, one structural trick. Order them from far to near (landscape → place → room → object), or
   from material to meaning. End on the thing that says the thesis most plainly.
3. **Facts.** Each line rests on a checked fact (a term, a technique, a measurement); write the source next to it.
   Names of techniques and parts are the best callouts (안허리곡 · 앙곡, 榫卯, 侘寂, flying buttress).
4. **Sets and objects.** For each chapter: the place (a set) and the one object the camera ends on. Sketch the camera:
   where it starts, where it ends, what it looks at.
5. **Colour and sound.** Which preset per chapter (paper for the opening is the signature), which accent materials;
   which scale (`gong`/`zhi`/`yu` Chinese, `pyeongjo`/`gyemyeonjo` Korean, `in`/`yo` Japanese, `dorian`/`hijaz` …),
   which mood per chapter.

## Stills rubric (score each contact sheet 0–2 per row; ship at ≥ 12 / 14)
1. Each chapter's first frame says its idea without the subtitle.
2. Ink stays ink: far things dissolve into paper or fog, never into black; no flat unlit colour.
3. Subtitles: short, one accent word, readable over the frame (pale on dark, ink on paper).
4. The camera is always moving, slowly; no frozen hold, no jump.
5. Callouts point at the named thing, inside the frame, clear of the subtitle.
6. Everything on screen belongs to this topic (nothing dragged in from the template, the example or the source film).
7. The card: stroke → title + seal → line, nothing overlapping.
