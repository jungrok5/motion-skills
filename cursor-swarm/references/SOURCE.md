What we took is the *idea and grammar* (written down below from frames sampled once a second). The site in the clip is
the author's own developer portfolio; nothing of it (name, banner, sections, copy) is reused.

| t (s) | what happens |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | black editor; a cluster of ~20 coloured arrows mills around one small icon in the middle; a pill at the bottom says "Generating…" |
| 1–2 | the cluster splits: cursors drag out small boxes with blue handles and size tags; the pill now reads `38 agents · 28 / 102 layers` with coloured dots and a progress chip |
| 2–4 | a full-width selection is dragged for the banner; a blue ruler band at the top; the name and title typed in a serif; "About" typed with a text frame; a blue `+` at each click |
| 4–6 | the banner fills in as pixel art (a stadium scene with pixel lettering); a paragraph is selected with a red frame |
| 6–10 | the page scrolls; paragraphs typed in parallel (several I-beams); a lone cursor carries a glowing "file" in the left margin; red horizontal guides cross the whole screen |
| 10–20 | "Projects": blue folder-like cards fly in from the left with thumbnails, are dropped into card slots and become screenshots; many small cursors around the cards |
| 20–30 | "Experience" and "Tech stack": rows of tool chips placed by a crowd of cursors; red and blue guides snap |
| 30–34 | "GitHub activity": the whole swarm swarms the contribution grid, cells lighting up in cursor colours |
| 34–40 | "Scrolled too far" footer: a button placed, the swarm scatters over the page; the pill flips to a published state |

The reveal works because (1) many agents visibly do different things at the same time, (2) every action uses a real
editor's language (selection, handles, size tags, guides, I-beams, drag-and-drop), (3) the page is clearly a real site
that grows top to bottom, and (4) the HUD keeps score. Those four are the fixed style of this skill (LOOK.md).
