# BRIEF — topic in, brief out (workflow step 1), and the reviews (steps 3 and 7)

Write the brief before any code, as `BRIEF.md` in the project (start from `templates/BRIEF.md`). It is what keeps a new
topic from turning into the template's girl and pet or the example's boy and umbrella: every line is about *this* topic.

## The brief

```
TITLE        a short, plain title (the film has no on-screen text; this is for the page and the file)
LOGLINE      one sentence: who wants what, what gets in the way, how it turns
HERO         silhouette in words — age, build, what makes the shape (hair mass, coat, trousers, a hat, a bag), 2 colours
COMPANION    an animal or small sidekick: species-ish, ears / tail / size, colour (often the paler value)
FORCE        what is against them, drawn how: wind, a scribble beast, a storm cloud, a closing door, a crowd, a clock …
PLACES       2–4: what each is (street / rooftop / kitchen / field / station), drawn with which kit pieces, what is new
ACCENT       the one warm colour and the one thing that carries it (and at most one echo in a background)
PROPS        what has to be designed for this topic (the kit has no umbrellas, kites, bicycles, cakes …)
STUNT        the one big physical moment (a leap, a catch, a fall, a flight) — with its anticipation and its landing
SCENE        5–9 numbered beats, one line each — the shots. Open on the blank page drawing the world on; end back on it.
SOUND        the tune's instrument and mood, the effects that sell the beats, where the music drops out
QUALITY      what every still must show (from the still review below, plus anything topic-specific)
MUST NOT     what would break the style or the story (clean vector look, saturated colour, a floating hero, …)
```

Design questions to answer in the brief, not in code:
- What makes the hero readable as a silhouette at 30 % size? (Big hair or a hat, a coat that flares, very wide or very
  narrow trousers, long legs.) Two people in one shot get different values (one light coat, one dark).
- What does the companion do in each beat? (It reacts first, it follows, it is left behind, it waits.)
- Which beat is the stunt, and what are its anticipation (crouch, wind-up), its arc and its landing (squash, puff)?
- What is unfinished on purpose here? (Walls washed only low, a skyline of flat blocks, the page itself.)

## Cast review (step 3 — on `cast.html`)
Score each person and critter 0–2 on each; fix anything under 2 before writing shots.
1. Silhouette reads at 30 % size and differs from everyone else in the cast.
2. Proportions in the style: legs about half the height, small torso, head ~1/7, hands and feet chunky.
3. Hair is a mass with a few locks breaking out, not a cap or spikes all round.
4. Clothes are oversized where the design says so and fold (knee folds, coat hem off the body).
5. Colours muted; no two garments of one person in the same value; skin reads against the paper.
6. Faces act: the 8 expressions on the sheet are distinct (brows and lids do the work, not just the mouth).
7. Turns hold together: front, three-quarter and profile look like the same person.
8. Critter gaits (sit, stand, run) read; ears and tail carry the mood.

## Still review (step 7 — on ~12 random full stills)
Each still must pass all of these; note the time and the fix for any that fail.
1. **Line**: rough pencil — doubled, overshooting at corners, varying weight; nothing looks vector-clean.
2. **Colour**: flat fills sitting a little off their lines; a shadow side on bodies and big shapes; colour stops short
   of the edges in backgrounds; the paper tone is visible somewhere in the frame.
3. **Palette**: muted (sage, olive, khaki, dusty teal, cream, graphite) with the one accent; nothing loud.
4. **Cast**: faces readable at this size; the pose says the action without the motion (key pose, not in-between mush);
   hands and props connect.
5. **Ground**: everyone standing has a contact shadow and stands on the line (no floating, no sinking).
6. **Frame**: the subject is clear; the hero's head and the story prop are not cut off when they matter; no plate
   edge visible; no empty frame outside the opening and closing page.
7. **Depth**: background lighter and lower contrast than the cast; scribble masses darker than anything else.
8. **Story**: you can tell which beat of the SCENE this is from the still alone.
